# Research findings

Inspiration, and a record of what yoga brands seem to agree on. **This is not a
blueprint:** what the site actually does is decided in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) and
[docs/DECISIONS.md](docs/DECISIONS.md). Treat a finding here as a prompt to think,
not a requirement.

Last reviewed 23 September 2026.

---

## The lens: what her business is

Most "yoga website" advice assumes a studio with a weekly timetable. Hers is
different, so read everything below through these facts:

- **flow4ward hosts events.** They are yoga, sometimes combined with horse riding
  or creative writing, anywhere in Romania. There is no class timetable, no
  membership, no teacher training and no fixed city.
- **Some events are collaborations** with other practitioners or venues.
- **The blog is a hobby for now,** but a large part of how she presents herself.
- **Visitors arrive from Instagram, on a phone,** often inside Instagram's in-app
  browser, which on iOS is Safari's engine (WebKit). Romanian comes first,
  English second.

## What yoga brands tend to agree on

### People choose a person

The sources agree on this more than anything else. Someone choosing a yoga
experience is choosing the person who leads it: her face, her story, her voice.
The examples that stand out read like her, not like a company:
- a handwritten touch;
- her own photographs;
- a first-person About page that explains her approach.

Generic stock photography and third-person copy work against this. (The
Cultivators case study, htmlBurger's examples, Komodo.)

### An event page is a story with the facts attached

The strongest retreat example in the sources, a single teacher's women's
retreat, was built as a "story-led" page that ends with the practical details:
- the price;
- what the rooms are like;
- FAQs.

The design studio that built it credits the page with the retreat selling out.
The pages for combined events (below) follow the same shape.

### Social proof

- **Testimonials with a photo and a name** are standard. For retreats, video
  testimonials from past participants come up often (htmlBurger); a short phone
  clip is enough.
- **Embedded Google reviews** are common on studio sites (Komodo; Magic Yoga uses
  a review widget). This needs a Google Business Profile first.
- **Invented proof hurts:** five identical stars on every quote reads as made up
  and devalues the real reviews.

### Few steps to book

**The target:** "three taps" to book, with guest checkout, no account, and a
booking form that looks like part of the site rather than a third-party widget
(Komodo).

**How Romanian camps often book instead (YogaSat's Danube Delta camp):**
- a WhatsApp message with your details;
- a **30% deposit** by bank transfer to hold the place;
- the daily programme sent on request.

The site's online checkout replaces all of that. Whether to take a deposit
instead of the full price is a question worth keeping in mind for multi-day
events.

### Questions that stall a booking

Beginners ask the same things before booking:
- "I've never done yoga, is that okay?"
- "Do I need to know anyone?"
- "What should I bring?"
- "What if I can't come?"

Good event pages answer them before they're asked. YogaSat's camp page
addresses the fear of not knowing the group directly. Omni Yoga has a whole
"beginner's guide" page.

### A blog in her own voice

For a studio, a blog is mainly a search tool: answer common questions and the
posts keep bringing people in (Komodo, Wellness Creatives). For a solo teacher
it is also her voice.
- **Omni Yoga's blog:** long, first-person, emotional essays. They are grouped
  mostly by feeling and theme (emotions, energy, philosophy, psychology), with
  only a handful about poses.
- **Magic Yoga's blog:** an archive of past workshops with guest teachers,
  largely photo galleries. That makes it a record of collaborations, which is
  worth noticing given how she works.

### Collaborations as reach

Retreat-marketing advice lists partnerships with other practitioners, venues and
local businesses as a main source of new people, alongside her existing
network (Wellness Creatives). A collaboration introduces each host to the
other's audience.

**Retreat marketplaces** such as BookRetreats bring bookings but take 10–30%
commission, which is why the sources call her own website "your most powerful
marketing asset".

### Visual conventions, and the risk of looking like everyone else

**The convention:** the wellness palette is sage, terracotta and cream, with
"two or three calm, natural tones and one accent for buttons" (Komodo). It is
still what everyone uses.

**The risk:** because everyone uses it, it reads as a template. The same goes
for the bubblegum pink (`#E8A0B4`) of countless wellness themes. The deeper
dried-rose this site moved to (`#A94E67`) is both more readable and less
obviously generic. Distinctiveness comes from her photographs, her words and
one confident choice, not from the palette.

**On a phone,** a full-width portrait photo is taller than the screen. Leading
with the headline, then the photo, tells a visitor where they are before
showing them a wall of image.

## Events that combine yoga with something else

Both of her combinations are established formats abroad. They are good
references for how to describe an event with two teachers and two activities.

**Yoga and horse riding:**
- Bitterroot Ranch (Wyoming, with yoga teacher Marc Holzman);
- Equescapes;
- Enchantment Equitreks;
- North Mountain Outfitter;
- a whole category on BookYogaRetreats.

**Yoga and creative writing:**
- Kripalu's "Ignite to Write";
- Sagrada Wellness ("Prose and Poses");
- Relax and Write retreats;
- On the Ground Retreats.

What these pages consistently state:
- **Who is it for:** "all levels", "no yoga or riding experience needed", with
  the riding level made explicit where it matters.
- **Who leads what:** the yoga teacher and the riding or writing teacher are
  named separately, with their credentials.
- **The daily rhythm:** yoga and breathwork in the morning, then writing
  workshops or trail rides.
- **Pressure removed:** "sharing your writing is optional".
- **What's included:** accommodation, meals, transport, group size.

## The Romanian landscape

Examined: Omni Yoga Studio (Oradea), Magic Yoga Joy Studio (Cluj-Napoca), the
camps of YogaSat (an independent teacher), and Retreat Romania, a two-host
retreat brand. Also Online Yoga, Altar Yoga (Bucharest) and the directory
Romanian Retreats.

- **The studios are class-first; events are extras.**
  - Omni Yoga lists its events as ordinary blog posts in an "Evenimente"
    category, each with a 📍 place and a 📅 date.
  - Magic Yoga's calendar holds 1,045 entries. Of the latest 200, about four in
    five are regular classes. The rest include workshops, several with visiting
    teachers, some offered both in the studio and online at once; a retreat; and
    a "Yoga si scris" (yoga and writing) session. So her writing format already
    has a local precedent.
  - A site built around events, like hers, is not the local norm, which is an
    opportunity to stand out.
- **The closest local match to her model is a collaboration.** Retreat Romania
  is "Pilates & Yoga Retreat pentru femei", run by two hosts, Andrada and
  Cristina, as weekend retreats at Romanian hotels. The site shows:
  - the next date and venue on its front page;
  - a sign-up button in the header;
  - participant testimonials.

  Its latest listed dates are from 2024.
- **Emoji do the formatting.** Omni Yoga, Magic Yoga and YogaSat all style event
  copy the way Instagram captions do: 🌿 as section markers, 🧘‍♀️ and 💫 as bullets, "– " typed
  as a list, 📍 and 📅 as labels. Her own text will likely arrive the same way,
  pasted from a caption.
- **Bilingual is common.** Omni Yoga runs Romanian and English and advertises
  Hungarian-language classes, which is natural in Oradea.
- **The legal pages are there:** Omni Yoga carries GDPR, cookie-policy,
  terms, house-rules and accessibility-statement pages, and a cookie-consent
  tool.
- **Directories exist.**
  - **Romanian Retreats:** aggregates retreats held in Romania and those run by
    Romanians abroad. Listing is "100% gratuit" (free), and it had 28 active
    listings when checked.
  - **BookRetreats:** lists English-language retreats held in Romania, for a
    commission.
  - A free listing is an easy extra channel.

## Blog and event text: Markdown or HTML?

**The question:** should her blog editor produce Markdown instead of HTML? An
earlier answer said no, because of the extra syntax. This section checks what
the rest of the web does.

### What 26 sites and three platforms actually store

| Site or platform | What it is | Built with | Articles are stored as | Event text is stored as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| omniyogastudio.ro | Studio, Oradea | WordPress 7.1 + Elementor 3.35 | Elementor JSON whose text widgets hold **HTML** | the same; events are blog posts |
| magic-yoga.ro | Studio, Cluj-Napoca | WordPress 7.1 + Elementor 4.3 + Modern Events Calendar | Elementor JSON with **HTML** inside | **HTML** from WordPress's classic editor; inline styles, `<br>`, emoji |
| Substack | Newsletter and blog platform | TipTap on ProseMirror | **ProseMirror JSON** while drafting, **HTML** when published | — |
| BookRetreats.com | Retreat marketplace | Custom Next.js | — | **HTML** strings |
| yogasat.ro | Independent teacher, camps | Drupal 9 | **HTML** | **HTML** |
| restograf.ro, onlineyoga.ro | Romanian blogs | WordPress block editor | **HTML** with block comments | — |
| retreatromania.ro | Two-host women's retreats | WordPress + Elementor | — | **HTML** in Elementor |
| yogawithadriene.com, meghancurrieyoga.com, elenabrower.com, mindbodycomplete.com, relaxandwriteretreats.blog | Solo teachers and retreats | WordPress block editor | **HTML** with block comments | — |
| yogajournal.com, kiamiller.com, asanavanessa.com | Magazine, teacher, yoga travel | WordPress | **HTML** | — |
| exhaleyogaretreats.com, stephaniesnyder.com, chelseykorus.com, trueflowyoga.com, sagradawellness.com, equescapes.com, enchantmentequitreks.com, onthegroundretreats.com | Retreats, teachers, combined events | Squarespace | **HTML** in text blocks | **HTML** in text blocks |
| kripalu.org | Retreat centre | Drupal 11 | **HTML** | **HTML** |

| WordPress (platform) | — | block editor | **HTML**, with each block marked by an HTML comment | — |

| Wix (platform) | — | Ricos | **Ricos JSON**, with converters to and from HTML and Markdown | — |

**How this was checked:**
- **Omni Yoga, Magic Yoga, BookRetreats and Substack:** read directly from their
  public APIs and page data.
- **The rest:** from the platform's fingerprint in each page (generator tags,
  asset paths) and the markup of recent posts.
- **The platform rows:** from each platform's own documentation.

### Substack in detail

- **The editor:** Substack's editor has been "TipTap on top of ProseMirror" since
  April 2022. That is the same editor library this site uses.
- **While drafting:** a draft is stored as a ProseMirror JSON document
  (`{"type": "doc", "content": [...]}`). This is visible in the widely used
  `python-substack` client, which builds drafts that way.
- **Once published:** the post is served as HTML. The public API returns a
  `body_html` field and nothing in Markdown. Custom pieces carry their settings
  as `data-attrs` on ordinary HTML elements: mentions, captioned images,
  callouts, galleries and buttons. That is how a ProseMirror document looks when
  written out as HTML.
- **Markdown:** Substack's help centre says "We do not currently support
  Markdown in the post editor" (updated August 2026). It does offer
  Markdown-style **typing shortcuts**:
  - `#`, `##` or `###` then space for a heading;
  - `-` or `*` for a list;
  - `>` for a quote;
  - `---` for a divider.

  These are TipTap's built-in input rules, which this site's editor has too.
- **Markdown as input only:** `python-substack` offers `from_markdown()`, which
  *converts* Markdown into Substack's JSON. Markdown is something you can feed
  in, not what Substack keeps.

### The consensus

**None of the 26 sites stores articles or event descriptions as Markdown.**
There are two camps:

- **Store HTML:** WordPress, Squarespace, Webflow, Drupal and the marketplaces.
- **Store a structured JSON document and render it to HTML:** Substack
  (ProseMirror), Ghost (Lexical), Wix (Ricos) and Elementor.

Markdown shows up only around the edges:
- as typing shortcuts (Substack, Ghost, TipTap);
- as an opt-in card for people who want it (Ghost);
- as an import and export format (Wix's converters, `python-substack`).

The sites where Markdown is the main format are the ones written by developers:
GitHub, dev.to, Hashnode and static site generators.

### Why this kind of writer doesn't get Markdown

- **The writers aren't developers.** They see formatting, not syntax. Markdown
  helps someone who wants to type plain text, and adds nothing for someone
  clicking a Bold button.
- **Markdown can't hold what these editors produce:**
  - YouTube and Instagram embeds;
  - video and audio;
  - image captions and sizes;
  - underline and alignment;
  - callouts and buttons.

  Every one of them falls back to raw HTML inside the Markdown, so the HTML is
  stored anyway, only in a harder-to-read wrapper.
- **Pasting is HTML.** Copying from Google Docs, Word or a browser carries
  formatting as HTML, and a rich editor turns it into structure. A Markdown box
  would either lose it or keep the tags.
- **It doesn't remove the security work.** Markdown renderers pass raw HTML
  through by default, so Markdown content has to be sanitized exactly like HTML.

### What that means here (for inspiration, not a decision)

- **Storing HTML puts this site in the largest camp,** with WordPress and
  Squarespace. The editor is the same one Substack uses, so the in-editor
  experience is already comparable.
- **Markdown-style shortcuts already work** in her editor: `##`, `-`, `>` and
  `---`.

  One gap: typing `# ` makes an H1. The menu hides H1 on purpose, but older posts
  still need it, so it stays in the editor's schema.
- **If Markdown is ever wanted,** TipTap's official `@tiptap/markdown` (3.31, the
  same version as the editor) parses and writes Markdown. It could accept pasted
  Markdown, or export posts, while storage stays as it is.
- **The other camp's approach is worth knowing: store ProseMirror JSON.**
  - **What it is:** Substack, Ghost and Wix keep a structured document.
    `@tiptap/static-renderer` renders one straight to React on the server, with
    no browser DOM.
  - **The benefit:** a post could then only contain what the editor's schema
    allows, which would take raw-HTML sanitizing out of the blog's path. That
    sanitizing is the step tied to the jsdom version that Vercel can't load.
  - **The costs:** converting every existing post, a renderer to write and
    maintain, and data that is harder to read in the database.
- **Event descriptions are the other half of the question.** Every site examined
  gives them rich text: paragraphs, bold, lists and emoji. Here they are plain
  text boxes whose line breaks disappear on the page. The research favours
  giving them the same editor as the blog.

## Search, as of September 2026

- **Event results are the valuable ones.**
  - **Eligibility:** Google shows its event experience only for events that are
    bookable by the general public and held in a physical place. Hers qualify.
  - **Required fields:** `name`, `startDate`, and a `location` with a name and
    address.
  - **Moving events:** because her events move around, each one needs its own
    real location. A fallback city would be a false fact.
- **FAQ rich results are gone.** Google limited them to government and health
  sites in 2023 and stopped showing them entirely on 7 May 2026. FAQs are now
  purely for visitors, and still worth it for that.
- **New in the EEA, which includes Romania:** structured-data carousels (beta).
  - **What they are:** an events list page marked up as an `ItemList` of at least
    three events.
  - **Where they appear:** "things to do" carousels.
  - **What that suggests:** a possible future use for the Events page.
- **Structured data changes how a listing looks, not where it ranks.**
- **Content that only appears after JavaScript runs may not exist for a
  crawler.** The event page was once rendered only in the browser, and its title,
  date, price and location were missing from the HTML.

## Instagram and WhatsApp

- **Stories show a link sticker, not a preview card.** The standard 1200×630
  share image does nothing there. It still matters wherever links are
  forwarded, above all WhatsApp, where Romanian groups coordinate.
- **The in-app browser on iOS is WebKit,** so it behaves like Safari, not Chrome.
  A security rule that Chrome ignored once broke the whole event page on iPhone.
- **Reels are 9:16.** An embed hardcoded to 16:9 letterboxes them badly.
  Instagram also only renders inside a frame from its `/embed` address; a pasted
  post URL renders nothing.

## Ideas considered and set aside

| Idea | Why not, for now |
|---|---|
| Newsletter / lead magnet | Ruled out by the instructor: nothing to send yet, and an unused signup form is worse than none. (Magic Yoga runs one; worth revisiting if the blog grows.) |
| Looping background video | Heavy on mobile data, and the audience is on phones. |
| Class-booking platforms (Mindbody, Momoyoga, Momence) | Built for recurring classes and memberships; her events fit a simple checkout. |
| Multiple instructor profiles | She is the only permanent teacher; collaborators are per event and belong on that event's page. |
| Google Reviews embed | Needs an established Google Business Profile first. |
| Blog view counts | Analytics already covers it, and it is for her, not for visitors. |
